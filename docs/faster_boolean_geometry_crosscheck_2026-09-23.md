# Faster Boolean Geometry Cross-check — 2026-09-23

## Scope

This is a synthetic same-operand regression, not a Spin-It Faster author case. The native evaluator's discrete design/quadric operands are passed to both the project's CGAL path and the pinned Cherchi probe. Five quadric fixtures are covered: ellipsoid, one-sheet hyperboloid, two-sheet hyperboloid, plane, and paraboloid. Each fixture runs twice, and the complete five-fixture batch was independently repeated in two output directories.

The exact-solid check uses `nef-exact-set-relations-and-symmetric-difference-volume-v2` on the two serialized output meshes. It now reports both exact represented-set equality and an exact-rational volume for the regularized symmetric difference. The volume is a measure, **not** a Hausdorff-distance bound and does not prove that the surface displacement is small.

## Difference-vertex localization follow-up

As a read-only follow-up, every CGAL output vertex was matched to its nearest Cherchi output vertex; the matches are bijective for all four unequal fixtures. All non-identical matched vertices lie on the unit-cube design-domain boundary (109/109 one-sheet hyperboloid, 65/65 two-sheet hyperboloid, 2/2 plane, 20/20 paraboloid). Maximum displacements are respectively `8.22e-15`, `7.85e-16`, `9.42e-16`, and `2.22e-16`; ellipsoid vertices are identical. The one-sheet maximum is at `(1,-0.39235619147297984,1)` versus `(1,-0.3923561914729716,1)`.

This localizes the observed numerical differences to boundary vertices, consistent with—but not proof of—different floating-point construction/rounding of Boolean intersections with the design-domain boundary. Some output face triangulations also differ (in particular for the hyperboloids), so vertex proximity alone cannot establish a surface-distance bound or exact solid equality. No geometry was snapped or rewritten. ## Operand-feature attribution follow-up

The next read-only probe enumerated exact-rational intersections between every unique edge of each serialized quadric operand and the six axis-aligned design-box boundary planes at coordinate `±1`. Each of the previously discrepant one-to-one output-vertex pairs was attributed to the same nearest candidate quadric-edge / design-plane intersection on both backends: 109/109 one-sheet, 65/65 two-sheet, 2/2 plane, and 20/20 paraboloid. The candidates were reconstructed from the exact rational values of the binary64 OBJ coordinates, without changing either output.

For this candidate set, the CGAL vertex equals the correctly rounded binary64 value of the rational intersection for 99/109 one-sheet vertices, 62/65 two-sheet vertices, and all plane/paraboloid vertices. None of the paired Cherchi vertices is bitwise equal to its candidate's rounded coordinate. This is evidence consistent with the observed sub-ulp-to-few-ulp boundary-coordinate differences arising during intersection construction. It does **not** yet prove that every candidate is the unique generating feature (some intersections can share or nearly share coordinates), nor does it establish a global error bound, exact-solid equivalence, or a defect in either library. The exact-set comparison remains unequal for the four fixtures. No snapping or tolerance change is justified by this probe.

Interpretation: record this synthetic reference comparison as an unresolved backend-rounding discrepancy, not as a discovered project defect. Continue solver integration against one explicitly selected discrete mass-moment/derivative map; the reference-backend comparison remains an independent blocked requirement until the exact set mismatch is explained or a paper-faithful backend choice is established.
